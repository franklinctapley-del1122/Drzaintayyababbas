# Global SEO and Content Rules
## Dr. Zain Tayyab Abbas Website

These rules apply to every future blog, specialty page, service page, location page, homepage update, SEO change, and publishing task in this repository. The source framework is the supplied Semantic Content Brief Blueprint, ChatGPT Leads Without Google case study, and ChatGPT Local SEO Audit Workbook. Adapt local-business tactics to a real medical specialist practice; never copy a tactic that does not fit the verified business model.

## 1. Non-negotiable principles
- Build for patients first: accurate, understandable, useful, safe, and easy to act on.
- Treat medical content as high-stakes YMYL. Never invent diagnoses, outcomes, credentials, patient stories, statistics, prices, availability, reviews, or guarantees.
- Separate documented facts, clinical guidance, practical recommendations, and uncertainty. Cite reliable clinical sources for medical claims and keep sources current.
- Do not claim that SEO, schema, AI visibility, or publication guarantees rankings, citations, appointments, or leads.
- Preserve the website's existing design system and working structure unless the user explicitly asks to change them. Do not modify unrelated homepage, header, mobile menu, or carousel behaviour during a content task.
- Never publish or mark a page medically reviewed unless the named clinician has actually reviewed and approved it.

## 2. Required content brief before writing
Every new target page must have a brief containing all seven components from the Semantic Content Brief Blueprint:
1. **Page identity:** exact title tag, URL slug (short, keyword-led, normally no more than five words), H1, search intent, Core/Outer role, audience and journey stage, priority.
2. **Query network:** primary query cluster, secondary clusters, question/PAA themes, long-tail modifiers, local intent where relevant, and competitor queries. Do not build a page around one keyword alone.
3. **Attribute filtration:** score candidate topics by Prominence, Relevance and Popularity. Use the supplied PPR formula: (Prominence × 0.40) + (Relevance × 0.40) + (Popularity × 0.20). Relevance and medical/source fit outweigh raw volume. Exclude unrelated high-volume topics.
4. **Contextual vector:** query-led H1/H2/H3 outline in logical dependency order. Every heading must answer a real query or patient need. Avoid duplicate intent and unnecessary headings.
5. **Contextual structure:** specify the right format for each section: definition, concise answer, paragraphs, bullets, numbered steps, comparison table, checklist, or FAQ. Word targets are section-specific guidance, not a ranking target.
6. **Contextual bridges:** plan upward, lateral and downward internal links. Record exact destination URL, descriptive anchor text and intended placement.
7. **Uniqueness additions:** identify one to three genuinely useful gaps after reviewing the top relevant results. Add original local context, clinician insight, practical decision support or a useful comparison only when verifiable.

If live query/competitor research is unavailable, label the query map and competitor gap as provisional. Never fabricate search volumes, PAA questions, rankings, competitor findings or PPR scores.

## 3. Article and page writing rules
- Start with a direct answer to the page's main question. The opening should quickly establish what the topic is, who it concerns, and what the reader should understand or do next.
- Use one clear H1. Use H2 for major query groups and H3 for genuine subtopics. Keep headings descriptive and natural.
- The Blueprint recommends at least two H3s beneath each H2. Apply this when it improves the hierarchy; do not create thin or artificial subheadings merely to satisfy a count.
- Place the CTA after the main educational/service content and before the FAQ block, where a CTA is appropriate.
- FAQ block comes after the CTA. Use at least eight FAQs when query research supports eight distinct questions. Every answer begins with a direct answer sentence.
- Do not pad content to hit 2,000–2,500 words. Completeness and intent coverage determine length.
- Use plain, professional English suitable for a general patient audience. Explain medical terms on first use. Avoid filler, generic AI openings, keyword stuffing, repetitive conclusions, and em dashes.
- Use tables only where they genuinely improve comparison or decision-making. Use lists for steps, symptoms and checklists.
- Avoid unsupported superlatives such as “best”, “No. 1”, “leading” or “top specialist”.

## 4. Medical trust and E-E-A-T
- Identify the author and clinical reviewer accurately when known. If clinical review has not happened, say so internally and do not add a false “medically reviewed” byline.
- Display a real publication date and update date. Update the date only when substantive content has been reviewed or changed.
- Include relevant clinician qualifications, role and affiliations only after verification and with wording that matches current evidence.
- Cite primary guidelines, recognised medical organisations, peer-reviewed research or authoritative hospitals. Link references directly.
- Explain when symptoms need professional assessment and when urgent care is appropriate. Do not diagnose the reader or imply that a home check rules out disease.
- Explain uncertainty, limitations, alternatives and individual variation. Do not overstate benefits, risks, test accuracy or treatment outcomes.
- Patient reviews, testimonials and case examples require authentic source material and appropriate permission. Do not invent or rewrite them to imply a clinical result.
- Add a medical information disclaimer where suitable, without using it as a substitute for accurate content.

## 5. On-page SEO and technical publishing
For every page:
- Unique title tag containing the main query/entity, written naturally and normally under 65 characters.
- Unique meta description that accurately previews the page and its value.
- Short, descriptive, keyword-led slug with hyphens; avoid changing established URLs without a redirect plan.
- Canonical URL must point to the intended indexable URL.
- One H1, logical heading hierarchy, descriptive image alt text, compressed responsive images and meaningful captions.
- Add relevant internal links with entity-specific anchors and links to authoritative external references.
- Include breadcrumbs where consistent with the site architecture.
- Add only appropriate structured data, such as Article/BlogPosting, BreadcrumbList, Person, Organization or LocalBusiness only when eligibility and real-world facts support it. Markup must match visible page content and validate. Do not add fabricated ratings, reviews, credentials, addresses or FAQ claims.
- FAQ content can be useful to readers; do not promise FAQ rich results.
- Ensure the page is included in the XML sitemap, returns HTTP 200, is indexable, has a self-consistent canonical, and is linked from relevant hub/list pages.
- Verify mobile layout, page performance, links, CTA buttons and forms before calling it ready.
- Keep key facts as crawlable HTML text, not only inside images, canvas, or scripts.

## 6. Local SEO and entity consistency
- Primary market is Lahore, Pakistan, unless the user specifies otherwise. Use Lahore naturally where local intent exists; do not append the city to every heading or force neighbourhood names.
- Keep the clinician/practice name, role, phone, address, contact details, hours and service information consistent across the website and genuine profiles.
- Distinguish a practitioner/clinic with a public patient-facing location from a service-area business. Never create a fake address, profile, branch or service area.
- Only create location pages when there is a real service and useful, distinct local information for that location. Avoid doorway pages and near-duplicate suburb pages.
- Maintain a NAP/entity matrix for website and relevant official profiles. Verify Google Business Profile and Bing Places eligibility and details before recommending edits.
- Encourage genuine reviews without incentives, gating, selective review requests or fabricated content. Respond factually and protect patient privacy.
- Show verifiable clinician identity, qualifications, practice location, services and contact routes. Link credentials to authoritative verification sources where available.

## 7. Search Everywhere Optimization and AI visibility
The supplied case study demonstrates one observed result, not a universal ranking formula. Use its practical lessons carefully:
- Make important pages discoverable in Google and Bing. Verify Google Search Console and Bing Webmaster Tools, submit clean sitemaps, inspect priority URLs and consider IndexNow for Bing-supported discovery.
- Check robots.txt, meta robots, X-Robots-Tag, CDN/firewall rules and HTTP status so legitimate search crawlers can access public pages. OAI-SearchBot search access and GPTBot training access are separate controls; do not change either without understanding the site's privacy and publishing requirements.
- Write self-contained answer passages that can be understood and quoted without surrounding context.
- Publish specific, accurate facts that help a patient decide: services offered, clinician credentials, location, appointment process, what a consultation includes, test/treatment explanations and limitations. Do not publish prices or availability unless confirmed.
- Create separate pages for genuinely different patient intents only when each page adds distinct value. Consolidate overlapping topics to avoid keyword cannibalisation.
- Keep titles, visible facts and structured data consistent and current.
- Do not treat llms.txt, schema, backlinks, or any single technical item as a guaranteed AI ranking factor. The workbook explicitly distinguishes documented platform guidance, observed processes and practical inference.
- Track ChatGPT referrals where analytics permits using utm_source=chatgpt.com, but understand that not every AI visit retains a referrer or UTM tag.

## 8. Measurement and audit cadence
Maintain a monthly fixed prompt set and record separately:
- Discovery: did the practice/site/page appear?
- Recommendation: was the practice actually recommended?
- Citation: was a specific page cited, and which URL?
Also record prompt, target location, competitors, sources used, answer position, missing facts, action owner and retest date. Do not combine these outcomes into one “AI visibility” score.

Review:
- Google and Bing impressions, clicks, query coverage and indexed priority URLs.
- AI referral sessions, calls, forms and qualified enquiries where measurable.
- Technical crawl access, broken links, redirects, sitemap and canonical consistency.
- Entity/NAP consistency, authentic review growth, credential verification and profile accuracy.
- Competitor gaps and real patient questions to inform the next content brief.

## 9. Pre-publish QA gate
A page is not ready until these are checked:
- [ ] Page identity and search intent are defined.
- [ ] Query clusters and competitor review are researched or clearly marked provisional.
- [ ] PPR decisions keep only relevant attributes.
- [ ] Every heading serves a query or patient need.
- [ ] Each section has an appropriate format and direct answer.
- [ ] One or more meaningful uniqueness additions are included.
- [ ] Internal links have exact URLs, anchors and placements.
- [ ] Medical claims have reliable sources and no unsupported promises.
- [ ] Author/reviewer attribution is truthful.
- [ ] Title, description, slug, canonical and H1 are correct and unique.
- [ ] Images are compressed and have descriptive alt text.
- [ ] Structured data is accurate, appropriate and validated.
- [ ] CTA and FAQ are in the correct order; FAQs are distinct and evidence-based.
- [ ] Sitemap, indexability, HTTP 200 and internal discovery are checked.
- [ ] Mobile layout, performance, links, forms and contact actions work.
- [ ] No placeholder text, invented testimonials, unsupported superlatives or inaccurate facts remain.

## 10. Workbook usage
Use the supplied ChatGPT Local SEO Audit Workbook as the operational audit system:
- Checklist sheet: 110 currently listed checks across crawl access, indexing, entity/NAP, profiles, service/location relevance, citation-ready content, independent trust, reviews, structured data, technical quality and monitoring.
- Use its statuses exactly: Not Started, In Progress, Partial, Done, Not Applicable. Priorities: Critical, High, Medium, Low.
- Keep evidence and notes for each status. Do not mark an item Done without verification.
- NAP Matrix: compare website and real official profiles for business identity, contact details, hours, services, location, credentials and review facts.
- Prompt Tests: use the same prompt set over time; record discovery, recommendation and citation separately.
- Competitor Audit: log competitors and the exact pages/sources shown.
- Sources: label claims as documented by OpenAI/Google/Bing, observed process, practical inference or technical audit method. Never present inference as an official ranking factor.

## 11. Change-control rule
For website implementation, change only the files and components needed for the requested task. Preserve existing layout and behaviour outside scope. Run the available build/type checks, inspect the diff, verify links and metadata, then report exactly what changed and what remains unverified. Never say a page is published/live until its production deployment is READY and the live URL has been checked.
